1. Customer Mobility Approval Process

Source: Customer Mobility Approval Process — May 2026 (CEC Guidelines)
Mandatory Task Routing Rule Customer Mobility Approval Process (May 2026) When a customer's vehicle is off-road (VOR) and they have requested mobility, ALL MOBILITY APPROVAL TASKS SHOULD BE ASSIGNED TO TONY DOUGHTY.

Key Operational Rules & Exceptions Customer Mobility Approval Process (May 2026)

  • Like-for-Like Policy: Nissan does not offer like-for-like vehicles as standard, but may consider a request if the customer is likely to incur business losses without a suitable vehicle (e.g., converted van).
  • Goodwill & Extended Warranty: Mobility is not provided for a goodwill extension or under an extended warranty, unless exceptional circumstances apply due to a Nissan delay (such as a parts delay or technical solution delay).
  • Exceptional Circumstances Chargeback: When Nissan agrees to keep a customer mobile in exceptional circumstances, there is no chargeback to the dealer.
  • Retrospective Reimbursements: If a customer rents a vehicle themselves without prior authorisation and requests reimbursement, it falls outside this process—refer to the QUICK REFERENCE GUIDE - BACS PROCESS.

Eligibility Pathways & Escalation Customer Mobility Approval Process (May 2026)

Path A: Customer IS Eligible for Mobility

Criteria: Vehicle is in warranty AND repair is warrantable (or exceptional request, e.g., VOR with no ETD on part).

  • Dealer provided CC or Enterprise rental: No CEC action required.
  • Dealer refuses to provide mobility: Follow escalation process within 8 business hours: (1) CQM 1 → (2) CQM 2 → (3) CQM 3 → (4) APM → (5) RBU cc National Aftersales Operations Manager / Regional Aftersales Manager (Template: NMGB Mobility Process Task Template FOR INFO).
  • Post-RBU Approval: Concurrently send the 100% chargeback email to the Dealership's CQM (cc APM) using Template CEC 10 and raise a New Rental Approval task (CEC 1).

Path B: Customer NOT Eligible / Specialist LCV

  • Not in warranty / non-warrantable (no Nissan delay): No CEC action required.
  • Dealer unable to provide suitable mobility (e.g., converted van) with anticipated business losses: CEC determines whether an Enterprise Outsource or Private Rental is the best solution for Nissan (cost) and customer (suitability).
  • Enterprise Outsource: Initiate a new rental on ARMS and define requirements in notes. Local branch secures an outsource quote with an expiry date—include this expiry date in the approval task and mark as 'urgent'.
  • Private Rental: Customer must provide a quote; make no commitment to cover costs until approved by NMGB via task.

3. RAC Out-of-Hours Recovery Case Creation

Source: NMGB – CS – Quick Reference Process Flow – RAC Recovery Case Creation (V.1, 2026.10.09)

Applies when a customer breaks down out of hours, is recovered by the RAC, and receives an Enterprise replacement vehicle for onward mobility. NMGB-CS RAC Recovery Flow V.1

First Working Day Mandatory Actions NMGB-CS RAC Recovery Flow V.1

  1. Locate Rental: Enterprise rental initiated by the RAC appears in the Open Complaint Tracker the working day following recovery under the 'RBU Rentals - No Case #' sheet.
  2. Create Case: Raise a Salesforce case and assign a Case Manager on the same day it appears in the Open Complaint Tracker.
  3. Contact Dealership: Phone the receiving dealership on the same day (followed by a task on the case) to inform them of the RAC replacement vehicle, advise that the customer may stay in it for up to 3 working days, and confirm the off-hire date.

Day 3 & Delayed Recovery Decision Paths NMGB-CS RAC Recovery Flow V.1

Customer IS Eligible After 3 Days

  • Dealership must provide one of their own courtesy cars OR extend the rental on the dealer ARMS account.
  • If dealer fails to act, follow the escalation process; if unsuccessful, request NMGB approval to extend on the RBU account with 100% chargeback to Dealer.
  • Close case once dealership assumes mobility responsibility (STOP SURVEY).

Customer NOT Eligible After 3 Days

  • Dealership must advise the customer that the Enterprise vehicle must be off-hired on Day 3.
  • Enterprise will contact the customer to return the vehicle or incur charges from the end of Day 3.
  • Close case when rental is off-hired (STOP SURVEY).
Vehicle Not Recovered to Dealership by 3rd Working Day (e.g., in storage) NMGB-CS RAC Recovery Flow V.1 CEC must escalate via email to RAC contacts Chris Babb (cbabb@rac.co.uk) and Jas Kaur Sangha (jkooner@rac.co.uk), request a rental extension on the RBU account, contact the customer, and manage the case until resolved and off-hired (SURVEY SENT upon closure).

4. Nissan Dealership & Authorised Repairer Mobility Guidelines

Source: Nissan Customer Mobility — Dealership & Authorised Repairer Guidelines (V4, Updated Jan 2026)

VOR Definition & Parts Ordering Classes (Bulletin AMB 23 45) Dealer Mobility Guidelines V4 (Jan 2026)

VOR (Vehicle Off-Road): Vehicle is immobile, undergoing diagnosis or repair over 1 calendar day, and recovered to a Nissan Dealer/AR by Roadside Assistance following an unexpected breakdown. Dealer Mobility Guidelines V4 (Jan 2026)

  • ZVOR Parts Order Class: Vehicle must be physically VOR at a Nissan Dealer/AR/affiliated bodyshop AND/OR customer is dissatisfied with repair lead time; part out of stock at Lutterworth/Amsterdam; vehicle <10 years old and not a grey import; excludes fast-moving service parts and service campaign parts; VIN and RO/CEC case number required in GASS.
  • ZEO Parts Order Class: Used for urgent orders not meeting ZVOR criteria (including vehicles ≥10 years old or grey imports).

RSA Recovery Refusal Policy (Bulletin AOB 25 D10) Dealer Mobility Guidelines V4 (Jan 2026)

£1,000 + VAT Refusal Charge Dealer Mobility Guidelines V4 (Jan 2026) Recoveries within a 62-mile radius inside working hours must not be refused. Refusing an RAC recovery incurs a £1,000 + VAT charge (billed monthly after Aftersales Field Team review), except where the dealer lacks physical ramp size (e.g., high-roof, LWB, or converted LCV) or extenuating circumstances apply (fire/flood). Workshop lead time is not a valid reason to refuse.

6. Nissan Courtesy Vehicle Agreement Terms & Conditions

Source: Nissan Motor (GB) Limited Courtesy Vehicle Legal Agreement (Clauses 1–12)

Legal agreement between the customer ("You") and Nissan Motor (GB) Limited (The Rivers Office Park, Denham Way, Maple Cross, Rickmansworth, Hertfordshire WD3 9YS), governed by English law. Courtesy Vehicle Agreement T&Cs

  • Period of Use (Clause 2): Runs from vehicle delivery until Nissan advises the customer's vehicle is ready for collection, or upon termination (1 week's written notice under Clause 8.2, or immediate termination for breach/insolvency under Clause 8.1).
  • Insurance & £250 Excess (Clauses 4.2–4.3): Nissan insures the vehicle for normal road use (excluding driver personal injury and personal property). Nissan may recover an excess of up to £250 for any claim not directly attributable to a third party, plus full losses arising from wilful misuse or negligence.
  • Roadside Assistance (Clause 5): Covered by Nissan Roadside Assistance — customer must call both Option 1 (0330 123 1231) and Customer Services Option 2 (9:00am–5:00pm).
  • Maintenance, Windscreens & Tyres (Clauses 6.1–6.6): Customer must maintain fluid levels, tyre pressures, and legal tread at their expense. Windscreen/glass replacement is paid by Nissan only if carried out by Autoglass on a like-for-like basis. Replacement tyres fitted by independent repairers must be new (no re-treads) and match Nissan's like-for-like spec, size, and speed rating.
  • Fines & Prohibited Uses (Clauses 3.5, 7.2, 7.5–7.8): Customer indemnifies Nissan against all parking, speeding, congestion, and traffic fines (plus admin fees). Prohibited: leaving keys in an unattended/running vehicle, smoking, window stickers, towing equipment or modifications without written consent, taxi/hire/driving instruction, racing, or taking the vehicle outside the UK without prior written consent.
  • Return & Inspection (Clause 9): Vehicle must be returned clean with all keys, spare wheel/tools, parcel shelf, and service history, meeting BVRLA fair wear and tear guidelines. Customer must pay immediately for damage beyond fair wear and tear or missing items.

8. Nissan Goodwill Process & CC2 / CCG Eligibility

Source: Nissan Goodwill Process — Dealer & AR Guidelines (Effective 22nd April 2025, V1)

Customer Support Cascading Hierarchy Goodwill Process Guidelines (Apr 2025)

  1. Manufacturer Warranty: Primary support mechanism for technical issues.
  2. Extended Warranty: Primary support mechanism if within commercial extended warranty scope.
  3. Goodwill Extension: For specific technical issues outside warranty/extended warranty periods.
  4. Technical Goodwill (Advanced CC2): Dealer-empowered parts contribution up to 7 years / 100,000 miles (or 160,000 km). CCN Dealer Goodwill Self Authority is removed. Customer pays labour capped at Warranty Labour Rate and Flat Rate Time (FRT).
  5. Commercial Goodwill (CCG): Exceptional cases only via Salesforce DCM case (gestures / exceptional labour contribution). Never used to cover dealer issues (lack of courtesy cars/technicians).

CC2 Eligibility Criteria & Validation Documents Goodwill Process Guidelines (Apr 2025)

Purchase Channel Eligibility Criteria Required Validation Documentation
Purchased from Nissan Maintained per Nissan Service Schedule at a Nissan Dealer/AR using Nissan Genuine/Approved parts; OR fleet customer owning 25+ Nissan vehicles (Sales Type C - Core, Leasing, Rental) maintained per schedule. Copy of periodic maintenance service records (plus sales invoice/evidence of ownership and eligible e-NEWS sales type for fleet).

Prior periodic maintenance service records + post-purchase Nissan service invoice, V5/sales invoice, or Nissan repair invoice.

CCG Exceptional Labour Contribution (Via DCM Case) Goodwill Process Guidelines (Apr 2025)

  • Qualifying Indicators (up to 7 years / 100,000 miles): High-risk repair with low part value (≤1/3 of total invoice) and high labour (>4 hours repair duration); multiple live Nissan owner household (min 2 vehicles <5 years old or ≥3 Nissans in household); or multiple repairs (min 2 others) within <1 year.
  • Mandatory Scoring Matrix: Dealer must attach a fully completed Scoring Matrix to the DCM case. Missing/incomplete matrices result in automatic case closure and an email to the CQM.
  • CCG Validation Docs: Sales invoice (bought new/in-network), job card (repair duration), Car Care Plan screenshot (live extended warranty or maintenance contract), V5/sales invoices (household Nissans in past 10 years).
  • Audit & 14-Day Rule: Dealers must convert e-NEWS goodwill queries to claims within 14 days of repair and input labour details accurately to pass audit.

9. Advanced Technical Goodwill (CC2) Case Decision Flow

Source: Advanced Technical GW Eligibility (CC2) Process Flowchart

Defines how Back Office (BO) and Front Office (FO) handle customer-raised vs. dealer-raised goodwill cases and standard case codification: Advanced Technical GW (CC2) Flowchart

  • Vehicle >7 years / 160,000 km: Out of scope → log under Vehicle failure // Technical Goodwill + OSC and communicate goodwill refusal to customer.
  • Customer-Raised Case (Eligible, CC2 Not Raised by Dealer): Log as Vehicle failure // Goodwill / Customer eligible but no CC-2 raised; CEC requests dealer to raise the CC2 claim and refers customer back to dealer for final answer.
  • Incorrect Dealer Referral: If dealer already made a final CC2 decision but wrongly referred the customer to CEC, log as Vehicle failure // Goodwill - Incorrect Dealer Referral and refer customer back to dealer.
  • Missing Matrix: If dealer opens a case without the required Matrix, log as Vehicle failure // Goodwill / No Matrix.
  • Standard Compliant Case / CCG Assessment: Log as Vehicle Failure // Technical Goodwill. If CC2 is raised and approved, CEC assesses CCG labour eligibility using the GW Matrix (stars): if eligible, advise dealer of CCG labour contribution; if ineligible, advise dealer and customer that the CC2 parts offer is full and final.

10. Commercial Gestures, Salesforce Codes & Authority Limits

Source: Nissan Goodwill Commercial Gestures Training Deck (Slides 1–19)

Approved Commercial Gestures & Golden Rules Commercial Gestures Deck (Slides 8–12, 17)

Available Gestures (Choose 1 only): Service Plans (Participative Maintenance Contract), Extended Warranty (Extended Warranty Contract), Nissan Accessories, Nissan Dealer Vouchers (Vouchers), or Expenses via BACS (not advised; fuel excluded; monthly finance payments require RBU validation). Commercial Gestures Deck

  • What to Do: Complete thorough investigation first; align every offer with the dealership in advance; confirm customer acceptance (follow verbal acceptance with confirmation email); if customer remains dissatisfied, escalate to supervisor before implementing—if validated unchanged, provide The Motor Ombudsman details.
  • What NOT to Do: Never combine commercial gestures with repair contributions (CC2/CCG/CCN) or give multiple gestures without supervisor validation; never compensate for RAC/AXA/dealer delays; never offer Service Plans/Extended Warranty if the customer already holds one; never pay gestures where customer is claiming mobility/business losses (assessed separately); never reimburse already-paid repair bills unless it is a GE campaign concern.
  • Wear & Tear Exclusions (CC2 & Commercial): Wheel balancing/alignment, engine tuning, headlight aiming, light bulbs (except HID/Xenons, which Nissan pays for), spark plugs, drive belts, clutch, brake discs/shoes/pads, filters, wiper blades, fluids/lubricants, and EV 12V battery (unless unreasonable failure on a very young vehicle).
